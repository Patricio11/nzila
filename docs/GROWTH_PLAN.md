# NZILA · HOW PLAYERS FIND THE GAME (v0.1, 2026-11-14)
**Companion to `ROADMAP.md` (Phases 4B, 14, 14B, 17) and decision D-0.35.**

> There is no marketing budget. This plan assumes none. Every channel below costs time, not money, unless it says otherwise. Numbers are targets to test, not forecasts.

---

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

A player in Luanda pays for every megabyte, so nobody tries a game because an advert said so. They try it because someone they know sent it, and because the message said what it would cost. So the game makes things worth sending (a BI card, the receipt of a first wage, a wedding photograph on the Marginal), each one carries a link, and the link puts the newcomer in the world beside the friend who sent it, as a guest, in under a minute. The diaspora is the second engine: people abroad who miss the place, have good phones and wifi, and want something to do with the family at home.

## 2. What the product must do for this to work

| Need | Where it is built |
|---|---|
| A link opens straight into the world as a guest, no email | 1.8, 2.1 |

If any row is missing, no channel below will carry.

## 3. Angola

1. **WhatsApp, first and mostly.** Status and groups are where links travel. The share card is built for a status: tall, one image, the link, the megabytes. A WhatsApp Channel for the game carries one post a week.
2. **Facebook.** The leading game in this market lives there, which shows the audience does. One page, one post a week at the start, always a real screenshot at golden hour.
3. **TikTok and Instagram, short video.** Fifteen seconds: the cobrador's call, the van pulling away, the bay. One clip a week from the first walkable build onward. Building in public starts in Phase 1, not at launch.
4. **Kuduro and semba producers we commission (D-0.18)** are our first partners with a following. Their loop plays in the game; they tell their audience where to hear it. This is agreed when the music is commissioned, in writing.
5. **The bairro, in person.** The founder's own circle for the first fifty. Then students: one university, one secondary school leaving year, one football team. A turma that joins together stays together.
6. **In the street, when there is money.** A sticker with a code inside real candongueiros. It is the most fitting advert this game could have, and it waits for Phase 14.
7. **A mobile operator.** Once the alpha shows megabytes per session and return rates, the founder asks the operators for zero-rated or discounted data for the game's domain. For the operator it is a youth product that costs them little. For us it removes the single biggest reason not to try. Nothing in the product depends on getting it.

8. **Players who film.** Creators already post their sessions of games like this in parts, unasked. We make that easy and flattering: a "Modo vídeo" in Definições that hides the wallet and other players' names, keeps the HUD clear of where the networks put their own buttons, and holds golden hour a little longer. We never pay for it and never script it.
9. **Artists whose songs play in the game (D-0.18).** Each licensed track is credited on the radio caption with the artist's link, and the artist has a reason to tell their audience where it plays. One signed artist is worth more than any post of ours.

## 4. The diaspora (Portugal, South Africa, Brazil, Namibia, then France and the United Kingdom)

**Why they come:** saudade. To stand on the Marginal at five in the afternoon from a flat in Lisbon or Johannesburg, and to be there with a cousin who is really in Luanda. Lisbon and Luanda keep nearly the same hour, and Johannesburg is one hour ahead, so evenings overlap.

1. **"Joga com a família."** The invite link is the product for this audience. The line on the card for them is about who is waiting, not about the game.
2. **Where they already gather:** Facebook groups of Angolans in each country, student associations, churches, Angolan restaurants and barbers in Lisbon, Porto and Johannesburg, community radio and podcasts. The founder is in South Africa: Johannesburg and Cape Town are the first test, in person.
3. **Dates that pull people home in their heads:** 11 de Novembro, Carnaval, Christmas and the new year. Each is an event in the world (10.6) and a reason to send the link to everyone abroad.
4. **They are the first paying players.** They hold cards, and the shop opens with cards (14B.2). **Oferecer** lets someone abroad give a cosmetic to a kamba at home (14B.3). It is a gift, never Makuta, never power.
5. **Later:** English and French catalogs (Phase 18) for the children of the diaspora who read Portuguese less easily.

## 5. Stages and what we look at

| Stage | When | What we do | What must be true to move on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Em construção | Phases 1 to 4 | One short clip a week. A WhatsApp Channel and one page per network. No promises, no dates. | A few hundred people following who asked to be told |
| 2. Alfa aberta | Phase 4B | The founder's fifty, then anyone with the link. Share cards live. | Day-1 return above 20%; at least one in five players sends a card; megabytes per session inside budget |
| 3. Beta fechada | Phase 14 | Invite waves as in the playbook. First diaspora test in South Africa and Portugal. | The playbook's numbers (day 1 above 35%, day 7 above 15%) |
| 4. Porta aberta | Phase 14, open | The launch event in the world. Commissioned producers post. Operator conversation in progress. | Each player brings more than half a new player on average |
| 5. Depois | 14B onward | The shop, Oferecer, stickers in vans, partners. | The game pays for its servers and moderators |

One number matters above the others at every stage: **of the people who open a link, how many take their first corrida.** If that is low, no amount of sharing helps, and the work is on the front door.

## 6. How the brand shows up outside the game

The leading game in this market has a consistent look in the feed: a cream ground, yellow highlight blocks, heavy black type, numbered lists. We must be as consistent and plainly not them.

- **Our ground is the world, not a flat colour.** Every post is a real screenshot at golden hour, or night blue (`--n-noite`) with one gold element. Never cream with a yellow highlight.
- **Our formats are our papers.** An update is a receipt ("O que chegou esta semana", line by line). A feature is a BI card. A new place is a stamp. Nobody else can post in that shape.
- **One voice.** The copy rules of `UX_UI_SPEC.md` §11: short, warm, "tu", Angolan words where they are natural, no exclamation marks from us.
- **Nothing before it is true (Rule 17).** No player counts we cannot show, no renders that are not the game on a phone, no "em breve" for things not yet in a phase plan.
- **We never name or answer another game**, in posts or in comments.
- A one-page brand sheet (logo, the two grounds, the three paper formats, type, do and do not) is made with the tokens in Task 0.7, before the first public post.

## 7. What this plan does not include, on purpose

- Paid installs. On a web game with a data cost they buy visits, not players.
- Rewards for inviting. They bring fake accounts, and the game refuses fake numbers.
- Influencers paid per post, until there is revenue and a way to measure what each brought.
