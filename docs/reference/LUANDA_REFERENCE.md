# NZILA · LUANDA REFERENCE NOTES (v0.1, 2026-11-14)
**Companion to `WORLD_ATLAS.md` §2 (district L1) and `UX_UI_SPEC.md` §0. Input to `docs/ART_BIBLE.md` (Task 0.7) and the L1 pack (Task 1.5).**

> **What this is.** Facts about the first district gathered from published sources, each turned into a decision for the game. **What it is not:** a substitute for the founder's eyes. Every line marked **[founder]** needs his yes or his correction, and every landmark still needs photographs he owns or may use (the art is modelled from those, never from pictures found online).
> **How it was gathered.** Text sources were read; image searches were run for the founder to look at. The notes below rest on the written descriptions, not on anyone's memory of a photo.

---

## 1. The Marginal and the bay

| What the sources say | What the game does with it |
|---|---|
| The Marginal is a gently curving, tree-lined avenue along the whole bay. The rebuilt promenade opened in 2012 and runs about 3.1 km. | L1 is a crescent. At postcard scale the promenade is about 400 m: a wide paved walk, a double row of palms, the road behind, the bay in front. The curve must be visible from either end; it is the district's shape. |
| The promenade has sports courts, a cycle path, restaurants, small shops, a space for concerts and a police post. | All six go in the pack: two courts (basketball and a five-a-side pitch) as places players gather; a cycle lane where a rented bicycle is the first vehicle a player ever uses; a kiosk row as the first commercial lots; a small stage for events (10.6); the police post as a heat input (the promenade stays calm at night). |
| About 2 500 people use it on a weekday and twice that at weekends. | The NPC crowd table is fuller on game weekends, and fullest in the hour before sunset. |
| It is where Luanda's couples go for wedding photographs: dozens of weddings a week, each with a convoy of fifty or more guests. | **A signature piece of ambient life nobody else has:** at golden hour on game weekends a wedding party arrives, the couple pose against the bay with a photographer, the guests in their best clothes, cars with ribbons. Players can watch, clap (an emote), and later hire the promenade for their own celebration. **[founder: how does a Luandan wedding convoy look and sound today?]** |
| Young people without work fish and gather shellfish in the bay for a living. | The pescador biscato is true to the place. It is shown as skilled work, with regulars on the wall who greet a returning player. |
| The bay's water was cleaned as part of the works; the promenade has many car parks. | Water in the bay is calm and teal, not dirty. Parking exists and matters once vehicles arrive (6.3). |

## 2. The pink bank building

| What the sources say | What the game does with it |
|---|---|
| Designed by the architect Vasco Regaleira, inaugurated in 1956. Pink and white, with a dome and colonnaded arcades. It fills an entire block of the Marginal. People call its look a "wedding cake". | The largest single landmark facing the bay. Three decisions for the model: (1) the pink is the strongest colour on the promenade and is kept pure; (2) the dome sits on the corner and is exaggerated slightly so it reads from the far end of the crescent; (3) the arcades are deep enough to walk under, with benches and a bookseller in their shade. |
| It houses the central bank, a state institution. | Architecture only. No name on the building, no emblem, no guards in real uniform (Rule 8). In the catalog it is "o edifício cor-de-rosa da Marginal". **[founder: is that what people would call it?]** |

## 3. The Fortaleza de São Miguel

| What the sources say | What the game does with it |
|---|---|
| Built in 1576, the oldest surviving building in Luanda, on high ground guarding the entrance to the bay. Thick walls with cannons. | The west end of the crescent rises to it. The walls are heavy, pale and plain, in deliberate contrast with the pink building. Cannons on the ramparts point at the bay. A path climbs to a public viewpoint outside the walls: the best view in the district, the place the arrival camera shows first. |
| Inside is the military history museum, with old statues and a collection of painted tiles telling the country's history. Entry is still controlled by soldiers. | The interior is not playable. The gate is closed with a sign giving opening hours in the world's voice. This avoids showing a military site and keeps the pack small. |
| The last Portuguese flag in Angola was lowered here at midnight on 11 November 1975. | The 11 de Novembro event (10.6) is held at the viewpoint: at game midnight the district gathers there. It is told with dignity and without party symbols. **[founder: how should Independence Day feel in the game?]** |

## 4. The Palácio de Ferro and the old churches

| What the sources say | What the game does with it |
|---|---|
| An iron building from the 1890s, attributed to the house of Gustave Eiffel, painted bright yellow, with metal filigree and a gallery running all the way round. Restored in 2009. In the Baixa. | One block back from the promenade. Yellow and filigree: the third colour note of the district after pink and fortress stone. The filigree is modelled as a few bold cut-out panels, not fine mesh (cheap to draw, reads at distance). The wrap-around gallery is walkable. |
| The Igreja de Nossa Senhora dos Remédios (1655) has a twin-towered front; the small Igreja da Nazaré sits among the towers. | One old church front appears in the inner street as architecture and a quiet square. No interior, no mechanics (atlas §7). |

## 5. Candongueiros

| What the sources say | What the game does with it |
|---|---|
| Painted blue and white; everyone calls them "azul e branco". | The livery is the game's transport colour (`--n-azul` on white). In speech and copy NPCs also say "o azul e branco". |
| A driver and a cobrador work each van. | The two-player job in 6.6 is true to life: driver and cobrador share the takings. |
| A ride is a "corrida", and routes are "linhas". In 2024 a corrida went up to 200 Kz. A corrida covers a fixed stretch (up to 16 km); long journeys are cut into several short linhas, each paid again, which passengers complain about. | The catalog says "corrida" and "linha", not "viagem" and "rota". One corrida costs a flat fare inside a district; crossing into the next district is a new corrida. That is both authentic and exactly how district gateways already work. The base fare tunable is set so a corrida feels like 200 Kz does. |
| Real linhas named in the press: Benfica–Mutamba, Viana–Mutamba, Cacuaco–Largo Primeiro de Maio; São Paulo as a destination. | The Mutamba is the hub, as in the game's L1. When later districts open, linhas are named in this pattern, end to end. **[founder: the call a cobrador shouts for each, word for word]** |
| Drivers start around 05:00 and finish around 19:00. | Vans thin out after dark. At night the kupapata and the táxi are how you get home, they cost more, and streets with fewer people warm up (heat). Transport and risk are tied together by the clock, as in the city. |
| Fuel prices squeeze what drivers take home. | Fuel is the driver's main cost in the game too (the sink in 6.4), so the job has a real margin to manage. |
| A transport survey lists "moto-táxi" and "kupapata" as separate things. | **Answered by the founder, 2026-11-14:** a kupapata is the three-wheeled cargo motorbike, and it is used as a taxi as well; people are also taxied by ordinary motorbike and by the blue and white van. In the game the kupapata carries loads (a geleira home from the shop) and passengers; the ordinary motorbike taxi is a separate, quicker ride for one. |

## 6. Light, weather and the calendar

| What the sources say | What the game does with it |
|---|---|
| Sunset in Luanda falls within about twenty minutes of 18:00 all year; sunrise between about 05:35 and 06:25. Days are close to twelve hours in every season. | The world clock needs no seasonal day length. Golden hour is 17:00 to 18:00 on the game clock, which is why the direction is called what it is. |
| Two seasons: the cacimbo, cool and dry, roughly May to August or September; and the hot, rainy season the rest of the year. Average about 25 °C. A cold ocean current keeps the coast cooler than its latitude suggests. | The game year has both. **Cacimbo:** a pale, hazy sky, softer shadows, people in jackets in the morning, cooler colour grading, no rain. **Rains:** tall clouds, short heavy downpours that send the street running for the arcades, puddles, a washed, saturated look afterwards. Rain waters the hortas (8B.1). Seasons are an ambient set in the pack, switched by the Estúdio calendar. |
| The sea is warm most of the year and noticeably cooler from July to September. | On the Ilha (L2), fewer NPC swimmers in the cacimbo. A small truth that locals will notice. |

## 6b. Markets

Researched on 2026-11-14 from the press (sources in §8). Real place names, which the game may use. Nothing here is scheduled; it is what a market district would be built from.

| What the sources say | What the game does with it |
|---|---|
| **Mercado do São Paulo**, in the bairro São Paulo, Sambizanga: one of the oldest markets in the capital. A formal municipal building with paid stalls, surrounded by one of the densest informal trading zones in the city. Clothing, shoes, hair and wigs, perfume, watches, furniture, toys and food, by the unit and wholesale; street sellers buy in bulk from the warehouses around it and resell. Rua Arreiou-Arreiou is its busiest street. | The founder named it (2026-11-14) as the place to look at for how people sell goods. It is the model for the game's market: a building of fixed stalls a player can rent, and the street outside where a bancada or a bacia sells the same goods bought wholesale inside (Tasks 4.3, 8B.3). Candidate for its own district after beta. **[founder: what is it like to buy there; what do sellers call out?]** |
| It was rehabilitated in 2022 (709 stalls, parking) and from 2023 the province moved sellers off the surrounding streets; by 2026 the pavements were largely cleared, with informal selling still present. | The pull between the stall inside and the street outside is real and current. The game shows both ways of selling and takes no side: no eviction mechanic, nobody chasing zungueiras. |
| **Roque Santeiro** (Sambizanga, 1991 to 2010), once called the largest open-air market in Africa; its replacement at **Panguila**, far to the north, never took its place. **Kikolo** (Cacuaco) is the wholesale food hub. | Roque Santeiro is remembered, not rebuilt: an older NPC may speak of it. |
| **Congolenses** (Rangel): phone and television repair. **Asa Branca** (Cazenga): frozen fish and second-hand clothes. **Mercado do 30** (Viana): the biggest wholesale market, produce from the provinces and live animals. **Catinton**: vegetables, fruit and fuba de bombó. **Benfica**: crafts, carvings and masks. **Mabunda** (Samba): fish, on the coast. | Each market is known for one thing. When a district with a market opens, that one thing is what it sells best and cheapest, which gives players a reason to cross the city. The 30 is where a player's lavra produce goes wholesale (8B.3). |

## 6c. Where people live (founder, 2026-11-14; checked against published sources)

The founder's correction: **most of Angola is houses, not blocks of flats.** Blocks belong to the centre of a city and to Kilamba. A town drawn as rows of apartment buildings is not Angola. The development district of Task 1.2 made exactly this mistake and must not be taken as a model.

| What the sources say | What the game does with it |
|---|---|
| Luanda is two cities: the planned one of colonial origin (the Baixa and the central bairros), and the self-built one around it. Published estimates put **three quarters to four fifths of Luandans in the self-built bairros** (the musseques). | Every district has a **housing mix** written in its brief before anything is modelled (`WORLD_ATLAS.md` §2b). Only the Baixa and Kilamba are mostly buildings. |
| The self-built house is single-storey, of cement block, often unplastered, with a roof of zinc sheet or fibre-cement held down with stones or tyres. Residents themselves tell a *casa de bloco* from a *casa de chapa* (sheet metal throughout), and the difference matters: block means having arrived. | Two house kits, not one, and a player's first home may be chapa and their second bloco: a ladder people already believe in. |
| The **quintal**, a yard behind a wall or fence, was the heart of the home: cooking, washing, eating, receiving. Density has shrunk it but not ended it. | A house is a plot: wall or fence, gate, quintal, house. The quintal is a room without a roof where players gather. Quintal parties (L7) need this. |
| Streets in these bairros are often unpaved red earth, narrow, with becos between plots; water comes by tank and bucket; a generator hums; a satellite dish sits on the zinc. | The kit has earth streets, becos, tanks, dishes, generators, a cantina with a serving hatch, a shipping-container shop. |
| **Kilamba**: about 750 blocks of 5 to 13 storeys, built 2008 to 2012, painted in a few clear colours by height, on wide straight avenues, with schools and shops planned in. First nearly empty, then the home of salaried professionals. | L5 is the one district where the tower kit is the whole picture: clean, regular, wide, a little too quiet. |
| **Talatona**: recent, planned, a place of gated condominiums with their own guards, pools and gardens, of vivendas behind high walls, of shopping centres and car dealers. | L4 is walls, gates, guards and what is behind them. The vivenda is its house. |
| **The Baixa**: the colonial core, with old two-storey sobrados, late-colonial modernist blocks with galleries and brise-soleil, and new glass towers on the Marginal. | L1 keeps buildings, but three kinds of them, not one. |

**Outside Luanda** the same holds more strongly: a provincial capital has a small centre of colonial and modern buildings and is otherwise houses. The provinces are planned in `WORLD_ATLAS.md` §3; each brief states its housing mix.

**The quintal is the backyard** (founder, 2026-11-14): behind the house, not in front of it.

**What this corrects in what was built on 2026-11-14.** The rehearsal town has pavements everywhere, trees in neat pits and no wires: it is tidier than any street in these pictures. The house kit needs the wall and gate at the street, the zinc roof with its overhang, the two-colour paint, the pole with its wires, the dish, the pots, the open drain and the sand verge. The van's blue must be this blue and its split at the window line.
