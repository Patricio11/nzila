# NZILA · LUANDA BAIRROS: STREET PATTERN, PLOTS, HOUSING, LANDMARKS (research, 2026-11-19)
**Companion to `LUANDA_REFERENCE.md` §6c and `WORLD_ATLAS.md` §2, 2b, 2c. Draft for the founder; nothing here is a decision until the founder says yes.** *(2026-11-19, later: the founder dropped Vila Flor and added Maculusso, with photos of Maculusso, Vila Alice and Nova Vida; §2 is kept as a stub so the numbers do not move.)*

> **Overview.** The eleven bairros fall into five kinds. **Planned centralidade:** Kilamba, a single state project built 2008 to 2012 on empty land, a grid of superblocks of 5 to 13 storey blocks. **Planned suburbs:** Nova Vida, a post-war state *urbanização* (from 2003) of numbered streets, loops and cul-de-sacs; and Vila Alice, a colonial-era residential suburb at the west end of Rangel, now filling with taller buildings. **Colonial centre:** Cidade Alta, the political acropolis on the plateau; Maculusso, the old musseque of the crosses that became a quarter of villas and now of towers; and Ingombota, the old Creole bairro that the 1940s to 1960s turned into the modern centre. **Old musseques and the inner ring:** Rangel, São Paulo (Sambizanga) and Catambor (Maianga), dense bairros that grew round the colonial city from the 1940s, part planned, mostly self-built, with becos; Cazenga, the older periphery to the east, a colonial-era grid on one side and organic musseque on the other, now the most densely populated municipality. **Post-war periphery:** Viana, where Luanda grew fastest after 2000, on self-built plots along long earth streets off the Estrada de Catete.
>
> **How it was gathered.** Web search and reading of Wikipedia (EN), Angolan press (Ver Angola, Expansão, ANGOP) and academic work (ISCTE, Universidade do Porto, London Met, Roma Tre, ISCPSI, TU Delft/IPHS). Several key texts (the Viana Atlas, the Mingas thesis on Catambor, the Mitchell report on the periphery) are behind bot walls and could not be read; where a fact rests on a search summary or a title alone, that is said. **[inferred]** marks what the sources do not state and I reasoned from the maps or from neighbouring facts. **[founder]** marks what needs the founder's eye. The map screenshots have **no scale bar**, so every distance read off them is an estimate and marked as such.

## 1. Kilamba (planned centralidade)

**1. What and where.** A new city about 30 km south of the centre, administrative seat of Belas municipality according to Wikipedia (the 2024 boundary reform may have changed this: unverified). Designed and built by the Chinese state company CITIC on a US$3.5 bn oil-backed credit line, 2008 to 2011/12. Nearly empty at first ("ghost town", 2012); prices cut from about US$125 000 to US$70 000 per T3 in 2013; about 80 000 people by 2015 and about 129 000 by 2019.

**2. Streets and plots.** A rectangle in roughly 1:2 proportion on an orthogonal grid of wide roads, split into four sectors by a main north-south axis and a secondary east-west one; the main axis is a promenade carrying most public functions and the widest green strip (Bona, IPHS 2018). The works included **50 km of roads, 662 public lamp posts, 34.8 km of storm drains and 82 696 m² of planting** (RSD 2022). The map confirms the superblock grid and the inner loop roads. **[inferred]** About 30 superblocks for about 710 buildings means about 24 buildings each, and 50 km of road fits superblocks of **about 350 to 450 m a side**; finished tar, kerbs, markings and lamps throughout (atlas §2b).

**3. What stands.** About 710 (Croese, decree report) or 750 (Wikipedia) apartment buildings of **5 to 13 storeys** (Wikipedia; Inhabitat says 5, 8 and 11; the BBC "eight-storey"), about 20 000 flats in phase 1 and 25 002 by 2017. Units T3, T3+1, T5. **Each block painted its own colour** so that neighbourhoods can be told apart. Founder's photo (§6d): cream blocks with yellow hipped roofs in clusters of four round a court. Building addresses read like "Edifício C19a" (a listing), suggesting a letter per quarteirão and a number per building **[inferred]**.

**4. Life and landmarks.** 24 crèches, 9 primary and 5 to 8 secondary schools, a health centre, 198 shops (some in block ground floors), sports pitches, green spaces, plots kept for hospitals. On the map: the Feira do Kilamba (a market, marked temporarily closed), a higher-education institute and a public services centre on the edge. Paragens **organised** (atlas §2c). Residents: young salaried families.

**5. Rules for the game.**
- Superblocks of about 400 × 400 m on a strict grid of 4-lane avenues with a median; one central boulevard with a linear park and the schools and civic buildings along it.
- Each superblock: a one-way loop road inside, 16 to 28 blocks of 5, 8, 11 or 13 storeys in clusters of four round a court, car parks between, one crèche or school, a court.
- One colour per superblock (from a palette of about six), roofs yellow; letter-plus-number signs on every building.
- Shops only on ground floors of avenue-facing blocks; no casas, no walls, no informal stalls inside the superblocks (sellers at the edges and at the feira).
- Lamp posts every 75 m or so on the avenues (662 over 50 km).

## 3. Viana (post-war periphery)

**1. What and where.** A municipality 15 to 30 km east of the centre; the town is dated 13 December 1963 (Wikipedia, uncited). It grew faster than anywhere in Luanda: **42.1 % a year from 2000 to 2008, about a million new people**, because it still had open land and people came from the rest of the city looking for plots (Viana Atlas 2014, via search summary). 865 863 people at the 2024 census (new boundaries). Industry (the Viana industrial zone, a steel works), the railway, and the resettlement towns of Zango.

**2. Streets and plots.** Growth is "mostly unplanned, by continuous self-build", with almost no planned public space (ULusófona study of Projeto Morar). The map confirms long, straight earth streets laid out plot by plot in blocks of different orientations, many dead-end stubs, off the Estrada de Catete dual carriageway. The Rua da Suave, the main local street on the map, is about **2.3 km** and was put out to tender for rehabilitation in 2021 (Ver Angola). Floods are the defining event: in December 2023 Viana had the most flooded homes in Luanda (**4 048**); rubbish blocks the drains. Where sewers are missing, ditches and streams take the waste (Atlas). Resettlement houses in Zango stand on **15 × 15 m plots** (three bedrooms); the smallest "Tchuna" houses are a **40 m² core on a 150 m² plot**, built to be extended (Moreira 2017).

**3. What stands.** Casas de bloco on plots, warehouses and factories (atlas §2b). Zango: rows of identical single-storey resettlement houses, then extended by their owners.

**4. Life and landmarks.** The CFL railway station on the line Musseques to Viana to Catete; the **Mercado do 30** at Km 30 (over 5 200 sellers in 12 sections, about 15 000 people a day; produce, live animals, rice, oil). On the map: private colégios, event halls (*salões de festa*, one with a pool), churches and a Kingdom Hall, fuel stations, transport yards. Paragens **not organised**: in February 2026 Viana moved two well-known boarding points, "Bombas dos Mutilados" and "Ponte Amarela" (Ver Angola headline): paragens named after a fuel station and a yellow bridge.

**5. Rules for the game.**
- A dual carriageway (2 + 2 lanes, median, lamp posts) as the spine; the only tar. Everything off it is earth.
- Off the spine: straight streets 8 to 12 m wide, 100 to 300 m apart, in patches of different angle; dead-end stubs 30 to 80 m into each block.
- Plots 15 × 15 m to 15 × 25 m, walled, house at the front, quintal behind; about a third unfinished (bare block, rebar on the roof).
- Along the spine: warehouses, a fuel station, a salão de festas, a colégio, workshops.
- Paragens named after a landmark (a fuel station, a bridge), not organised.

## 4. Nova Vida (planned suburb, post-war)

**1. What and where.** An *urbanização* in Kilamba Kiaxi municipality, a state housing programme begun in 2003 with about 2 500 homes planned, for civil servants, demobilised soldiers and others (a phase II of 3 200 more is reported by one source only). Building started around 2006 according to Amnesty; its expansion involved evictions of informal settlements (2004 to 2006, HRW, IRIN). Today it houses tens of thousands, including senior officials (Expansão).

**2. Streets and plots.** **Numbered streets**, confirmed by press and map (Rua 53, "a rua dos bancos"; Ruas 111, 112, 153). About **54.4 km of roads, 55.7 km of storm drains, 96.6 km of water mains**, a sewage works (official figures for the rehabilitation). Twenty years on the roads are **potholed and the drains failed**: a US$259 m (2023) plus €208 m (2026) rehabilitation started in June 2026 and by late 2026 only three streets had reopened. The map shows a planned but not gridded layout: groups of short parallel streets at several angles, loops and cul-de-sacs, a few collectors.

**What the founder's photos show (95 to 97).**
- **95:** An aerial view along a **long, straight, tarred street lined with trees and palms**, with cars parked at the kerbs and a mast on the horizon. On both sides stand **walled vivendas**, one and two storeys, with **hipped orange tile roofs** and walls in cream, yellow or white. Several have a **swimming pool** and a lawn behind a solid wall, some walls painted terracotta. No two houses are alike, though the plot widths look even. More parallel streets of the same kind lie to the left.
- **96:** A listing photo (captioned "…T3 no Projeto Nova Vida"). A **four-storey block in pale yellow** with white trim and a **hipped pale blue-grey roof** stands behind a hedged **parking court** off a wide street. Beside it are **pink blocks with red roofs**, and further off, rows of similar blocks in pastel colours.
- **97:** An aerial view looking down on a **condominium of 4 and 5 storey blocks**. Their walls are white, grey or cream and their **hipped terracotta roofs** break into several sections over a stepped plan. They stand in **pairs and small clusters at varying angles**, on **lawns** with trees, hedges and paths, with a round structure among them (a water tank or a kiosk **[inferred]**). Blocks in pink and cream are visible in the distance.

**3. What stands.** The founder's photos answer the question: Nova Vida has two kinds of housing in their own zones **[the separation is inferred from the photos]**.
- **Vivendas on plots** line long straight streets planted with trees. They are one or two storeys with hipped orange tile roofs and solid walls, many with a pool and lawn. They began as a few standard designs and their owners have since made them different from one another (photo 95).
- **Condominiums of low apartment blocks** hold four- and five-storey blocks of **T3** flats in pastel colours with hipped roofs, standing in lawns with parking courts (photos 96 and 97). One source, a project brief of unknown author, describes **Nova Vida II** as **10 four-storey buildings with 160 T3 flats**, which is 4 flats a floor. A Phase II of about 3 200 homes on top of the first 2 500 is reported (World Folio). A condominium on the north edge appears on the map.

**4. Life and landmarks.** Banks along Rua 53; a shopping centre and a supermarket at the north end; two higher-education institutes; a clinic; event halls; a discount store on the south edge. The **paragem** was turned into a mural in 2020: earth replaced by gravel, benches for passengers, a wall painted by street artists (Ver Angola): a semi-organised paragem with art.

**5. Rules for the game.**
- Numbered streets only (Rua 1 to Rua 170), with a street sign at every corner and one "rua dos bancos" collector with shopfronts.
- **Vivenda zone:** long straight streets 12 to 15 m wide with a row of trees or palms on each side. Plots about 15 × 25 m **[inferred]**, each with a solid wall (cream, white or terracotta), a gate, and a one or two storey house with a hipped orange tile roof. About one plot in three has a pool **[inferred from photo 95]**. Start from two or three standard designs, then personalise: added floor, new wall colour, different gate.
- **Condominium zone:** blocks of 4 to 5 storeys (16 to 20 T3 flats each) in pairs and clusters of 3 to 6, turned to varying angles, on lawns with hedges and footpaths. A hedged parking court on the street side and one entrance gate with a guard hut. Each condominium takes one pastel colour (pale yellow, pink, cream, white or grey) and one roof colour (terracotta, red or pale blue-grey).
- Tar with kerbs and lamp posts, but potholed: patched sections, and puddles that stay after rain (until the 2026 rehabilitation reaches each street).
- One paragem with a painted wall and benches.

Sources.** [Expansão, rehabilitation starts](https://www.expansao.co.ao/angola/detalhe/reabilitacao-das-infraestruturas-do-nova-vida-arranca-tres-anos-depois-de-autorizada-73958.html) · [Expansão, three streets reopened](https://expansao.co.ao/angola/detalhe/centenas-de-milhoes-autorizados-e-apenas-tres-ruas-reabertas-na-nova-vida-75670.html) · [Ver Angola 2026, CGD loan](https://www.verangola.net/va/en/012026/RealEstate/47391/Government-requests-loan-from-Portuguese-bank-to-rehabilitate-the-Nova-Vida-urbanization-project.htm) · [Forbes África Lusófona, figures](https://forbesafricalusofona.com/?p=167659) · [Ver Angola 2020, paragem mural](https://www.verangola.net/va/pt/082020/Cultura/21603/Paragem-de-táxis-do-Nova-Vida-passa-a-mural-de-arte-urbana.htm) · [HRW 2007](https://www.hrw.org/legacy/portuguese/reports/angola0507/11.htm) · [Amnesty USA](https://amnestyusa.org/victories/angola-rehouses-victims-of-forced-evictions) · [The New Humanitarian](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/node/226082) · [The World Folio, Nova Vida 2 500 homes and Phase II](https://theworldfolio.com/news/housing-developments/3996/) · [Programa Base, Nova Vida II (Scribd, author unknown, unverified)](https://pt.scribd.com/document/996461595/PROGRAMA-BASE)

## 5. Cazenga (old periphery, musseque and colonial grid)

**1. What and where.** A municipality east of Rangel, 33.1 km² (2024 boundaries), **892 401 people in 2014**, about 1.05 million estimated for 2019: the most densely populated municipality in Luanda (Wikipedia). Originally farming villages where cassava was grown and made into flour, later swallowed as musseques (older residents, Estudo Prévio 2021); already a "slum" where water was sold by the can in 1974 (Bender). Home of the Luanda railway workshops and, with Sambizanga and Viana, of the city's industry (Guimarães).

**2. Streets and plots.** The map shows the two textures clearly: a **colonial-era grid of long narrow blocks** in the west and south-west, and **organic lanes** in the centre and east **[the dating of the grid is inferred]**. Development Workshop describes houses "put up without order" with precarious sanitation. **Floods** are central: 35 critical points named in one report (Asa Branca among them); in April 2026 Cazenga was the worst hit, with 3 163 homes flooded in Calawenda, Kima Kieza and Sinef. A requalification of Cazenga, Sambizanga and Rangel began in 2010 (drainage channels, structural roads, new blocks for rehousing); residents were told it would take at least 15 years.

**3. What stands.** Casas de bloco and de chapa with quintais, earth streets, becos (atlas §2b). **[inferred]** Along the grid streets older plastered houses with a strip of tar; in the organic part smaller houses packed wall to wall; some new 4 to 5 storey rehousing blocks from the requalification.

**4. Life and landmarks.** **Mercado Asa Branca**: four halls and open areas, over 110 cold rooms of frozen fish from five provinces, second-hand clothes from 300 to 10 000 Kz, a wedding-dress row (Ver Angola 2023). Hospital Geral dos Cajueiros, Hospital Municipal do Cazenga (map). The **Marco Histórico do Cazenga** (2005), a 24 m monument with two 6 m statues, to be restored (2025). A drainage vala across the south. Paragens **not organised**.

**5. Rules for the game.**
- Two zones in one district: a grid zone (blocks about 40 × 150 m, streets 8 to 10 m, one tarred street per four) and an organic zone (lanes 2 to 5 m, becos 1 to 2 m).
- Plots small: 100 to 250 m², houses touching on two sides, quintal 30 to 80 m².
- One open vala (concrete channel, about 4 m wide) crossing the district, with footbridges; low spots that flood in every rain event.
- The market as the heart: halls plus a ring of stalls, cold rooms humming, clothes in heaps.
- One earth football pitch per 500 m or so, ringed by spectators.

**6. ## 6. Cidade Alta (colonial centre, the upper town)

**1. What and where.** The plateau above the Baixa, in Ingombota. From the start the city's "political acropolis": a hospital, churches and administration, while trade sat below (Guimarães). Now the seat of the main state institutions: the presidential palace, ministries, near the Assembleia and the fortress.

**2. Streets and plots.** Few streets, large plots, buildings set round squares; the map shows it as a sparse knot at the west edge. **Restricted zones where photography is not allowed** (Ver Angola). The old railway once climbed to a Cidade Alta station; it no longer does. **[inferred]** Finished streets, calçada, lamps, guard posts, little traffic.

**3. What stands.** The **palace**: works 1607 to 1611 on what was then the Largo da Feira, one of the main slave markets; almost destroyed in 1761 and rebuilt in the Pombaline style; arcade and pediment added 1940 to 1950; classified national heritage in 2022. The **Igreja de Jesus** (Jesuit church, begun 1607 or 1612, finished 1636), baroque front; the city's founder is buried there. A square facing both where a statue stood from 1874 to 1975; the inscription stayed. Colonial-style public buildings throughout.

**4. Life and landmarks.** Civil servants, guards, visitors; views over the bay and the Atlantic; a park with elevated views is mentioned by one guide. Hospital Josina Machel at its foot (map). Paragens **organised** (as L1).

**5. Rules for the game.** Architecture only (atlas §7, Rule 8).
- A plateau 30 to 40 m above the Baixa, reached by two climbing streets and a stair.
- One large square of calçada with an empty plinth; on one side an arcaded, pedimented palace front (no name, no flag of office, gates closed, a sentry box without insignia); on the other a white baroque church front (architecture only, no interior).
- Two or three streets of two-storey colonial public buildings, pastel, with tall shuttered windows; no shops, no stalls.
- A public viewpoint at the edge looking down on the Baixa and the bay.
- Low heat, few NPCs, very quiet at night.

**6. ## 7. Ingombota (colonial centre, the old Creole bairro)

**1. What and where.** Both the district holding the CBD (14.9 km², about 145 000 people, "the oldest district") and the old bairro of the Ingombotas between the Baixa and the Kinaxixi, which the founder circled. The bairro grew in the second half of the 17th century from a camp of runaway slaves and the Igreja do Rosário; the name comes from Kimbundu *ngombo* (fugitive) + *kuta* (to settle). It was the home of the old Creole elite, a place of parties; from 1869 poor residents were removed, and from the mid-1940s Portuguese settlers pushed the Angolan families out to the Bairro Operário, built new streets and renamed old ones (Nascimento 2015).

**2. Streets and plots.** In the 1930s "certain streets still of stone", close to the asphalt; houses "definitive, others of pau-a-pique, others of meia-água" (a resident quoted by Nascimento). A 1925 photo shows open ground; by 1969 it was built up with apartment buildings. The map shows a dense irregular grid of short blocks (**[inferred]** 50 to 100 m) cut by diagonal avenues, and small parks. Neighbouring Maculusso has a formal grid of paved, tree-lined streets.

**3. What stands.** Colonial-era buildings, many restored (Wikipedia); late-colonial modernist blocks of the 1950s to 1970s (ISCTE, in `LUANDA_REFERENCE.md` §6c); new towers. The Kinaxixe modernist market was demolished (2008) for a shopping centre; three unfinished towers stand there.

**4. Life and landmarks.** The **Largo do Kinaxixi**, reopened on 11 November 2024 with the **statue of Rainha Njinga** back after ten years, traffic lights and green space. The Igreja do Carmo; Parque José Martí; Hospital Militar Central; embassies; hotels; offices; museums. Street sellers, motorbikes, vans. Paragens **organised**.

**5. Rules for the game.**
- Irregular grid of 60 to 100 m blocks, streets 10 to 14 m with pavements, two diagonal avenues meeting at a largo with a statue on a roundabout.
- Mixed fabric on every block: two-storey colonial houses with balconies, 6 to 12 storey modernist blocks with galleries and brise-soleil, one or two glass towers.
- Small parks with benches; a church front on a small square.
- One plot of unfinished towers behind a hoarding.
- Overlap: this sits between L1 (Baixa) and Maianga; decide where the boundary runs. **[founder]**

## 8. São Paulo (inner ring, Sambizanga)

**1. What and where.** A bairro of the Sambizanga district (Wikipedia lists it with Bairro Operário, Miramar, Neves Bendinha, Bairro Popular), just north of Ingombota. Sambizanga as a whole is about 14.5 km² and 244 000 people; it sheltered and organised the anti-colonial movements and was where Cape Verdean, São Tomean and Congolese newcomers first arrived. No history of the bairro itself was found.

**2. Streets and plots.** The map shows a tighter, more regular grid than Ingombota off Av. N'Gola Kiluanje and Rua Ndunduma. The street everyone knows is the **rua do arreiou-arreiou** (also "entrada da polícia"), so packed that cars can barely pass; sellers occupy road, pavement and the entrances of shops and buildings; residents must park far from home; trucks struggle to unload; at corners rubbish and smells (Expansão). From 2023 the province moved sellers off the streets, with partial effect.

**3. What stands.** Wholesale shops and **armazéns** round the market, residential **buildings with entrances** (prédios) on the same streets, a fuel station that roped off its forecourt. **[inferred]** Two to five storeys, shops below, flats above, plus older single-storey houses behind.

**4. Life and landmarks.** The **Mercado do São Paulo**: one of the oldest in the city, rehabilitated in 2022, reopened with **709 stalls and a car park** (the car park took sellers' space). Goods: clothes, shoes, costume jewellery, hair and wigs, furniture, toys, food; street sellers buy wholesale inside and resell outside, and drop prices at day's end ("arreiou"). Already in `LUANDA_REFERENCE.md` §6b. Paragens **not organised**.

**5. Rules for the game.**
- A market block (a two-storey hall with stalls, a car park beside) at the centre of a grid of 60 × 80 m blocks.
- The four streets round it: 3 to 5 storey buildings with shops and armazéns on the ground floor and flats above, roller shutters, crates on the pavement.
- One "arreiou" street where stalls and bacias fill the road and only people pass; vans stop at its mouth.
- Behind the commercial ring: houses with quintais, quieter.
- Day/night swing: the busiest street in the game by day, shuttered and empty at night.

## 10. Rangel (inner ring, musseque and colonial housing)

**1. What and where.** One of the six urban districts of the old municipality of Luanda: 6.2 km², about 261 000 people in 2006, **about 42 000 per km²**, one of the oldest and most urbanised (Wikipedia). In the colonial period a suburban zone where the most discriminated populations lived. Contains Terra Nova, Precol, Combatentes, Valódia, Vila Alice, Indígena, Zangado, Nelito Soares, Saiotes, CTT, Margal (probably Marçal).

**2. Streets and plots.** **Av. Hoji ya Henda splits it**: the south more formally built, with big sports and medical institutions; the north largely musseque (Wikipedia). Bounded by Av. Deolinda Rodrigues (south) and Av. N'Gola Kiluanje (north). The map shows streets named by letter and number (R. B5, R. B7). The **Bairro Indígena** was a colonial experiment of the 1950s: **phase 1 single-storey houses, phase 2 two-storey housing blocks**; an observer noted "the verandas were full of women and children, used to living outside" (Moreira, citing Monteiro 1973). The construction firm Precol built housing complexes for the colonial middle class, including one called CTT (1968) **[the CTT in Rangel's list is inferred to be it]**.

**3. What stands.** A mix: old colonial housing estates (rows of single-storey houses, two-storey blocks with verandas), casas de bloco and chapa with quintais in the north, the Cidadela towers on the avenue.

**4. Life and landmarks.** **Mercado dos Congolenses**: the biggest concentration of phone and TV repair "clinics" in Luanda, many run by Congolese technicians; closed 22 June 2026 for a six-month rebuild. Hospital Américo Boavida (1958); the Cidadela sports pavilions; the telecommunications institute; the Estação dos Musseques on the railway; the Largo 1º de Maio at the edge (where it belongs is open: see §12 and §13). A project planted a thousand trees (2020). The Escola do Pica-Pau reopened (2021). Paragens **not organised**.

**5. Rules for the game.**
- An avenue (2 + 2, median) through the middle; south of it a formal side, north of it a musseque side.
- South: rows of identical single-storey colonial houses on 10 × 20 m plots and two-storey blocks with long verandas, on a grid of lettered streets (Rua B1 to B9).
- North: organic lanes and becos, casas de bloco and chapa, quintais.
- A repair market of small stalls with screens and circuit boards; a big hospital front; a sports pavilion.
- Trees along the avenue.

## 11. Catambor (old musseque, Maianga)

**1. What and where.** A bairro of the Maianga district, by Av. Comandante Gika (map). Maianga's north is formal (paved streets, services); its south and east are mostly unpermitted building with **dirt lanes** (Wikipedia). Catambor is the main case of Ângela Mingas's 2024 doctoral thesis on the **morphology of the musseques**: about fifty years of growth (from the late 1970s), house types from the single cell to grouped houses, and **street types** (repository summary; full text not readable).

**2. Streets and plots.** A 2020 police-academy dissertation found the bairro is built **"mostly of becos" with "little public lighting"**, which makes it a main source of insecurity, and proposed lighting the streets with the residents and a police post. **[inferred]** Becos 1 to 2 m wide, a few through lanes 4 to 6 m, earth or broken concrete. For comparison, houses in Chicala (another musseque) measured by Moreira had **75 to 272 m² of quintal and 105 to 315 m² covered**; "a house with no courtyard is not the house of self-respecting people" (Redinha 1964).

**3. What stands.** **[inferred]** Single- and two-storey casas de bloco packed wall to wall, some upper floors added, quintais shrunk to courts.

**4. Life and landmarks.** Not described. A hotel and Av. Comandante Gika at its edge (map); the formal city of Alvalade and Maianga next door. **[founder: what is Catambor known for?]**

**5. Rules for the game.**
- A pocket of becos right beside formal tarred avenues: the contrast is the point.
- A network of 1 to 2 m becos branching off 2 or 3 lanes of 4 to 6 m; corners turn often, sightlines under 30 m.
- Houses touching on three sides, quintais 20 to 60 m², stairs up to added rooms.
- Lighting is the heat mechanic: dark becos heat up at night and cool when a player or a resident puts up a lamp (the dissertation's own proposal), never a label (atlas L7, Rule 7).
- Shops at beco mouths on the avenue.

## 13. Across all eleven (findings worth recording)

- **Event halls** (*salões de festa*) appear on three of the six maps: a kind of business every bairro outside the centre has. A generic one belongs in the kit.
- **Flooding** is the shared event of Viana, Cazenga and Nova Vida: ravines, standing water, blocked drains. A rain event that changes the street for a game day would be true to life.
- **Paragens named after a landmark** (a fuel station, a yellow bridge) is how the unorganised kind is named.
- **Sensitivities (atlas §7).** Cidade Alta is a state precinct: architecture only, no names, no access.
